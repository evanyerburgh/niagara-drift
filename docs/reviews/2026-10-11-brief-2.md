# fm1-drone: briefing for an independent design review

You are an experienced drone-synth player and sound designer: years with instruments like the
Stylophone CPM DS-2, Moog Subharmonicon, Soma Lyra-8, shruti box, tanpura and modular drone patches,
and performing long-form drone and ambient music. You're reviewing **fm1-drone**, open-source
firmware that turns the **M-VAVE FM-1** into a drone synthesizer. Judge it as that player would.
Be unbiased: say plainly what doesn't work, and what does.

Code: `~/fm1-drone`. The player's view, including the full control map, is `README.md` ("Playing
it"). Today is 2026-10-11. Nothing has run on the real hardware yet.

---

## 1. Reference instruments (what a drone player brings to it)

**Stylophone CPM DS-2** (the main inspiration). An analog drone synth with every control on the panel,
no screen. Two identical voices, each: a pitch knob (27 Hz to 1.6 kHz, continuous), X-WAVE (one knob
blending square, triangle and saw), PWM, OSC MIX to a square sub at -1 or -2 octaves, a 12 dB LP/HP
filter with resonance to self-oscillation, bipolar LFO depths for the oscillator (pitch or X-WAVE) and
the filter, and a volume that pushes into overdrive. Between the voices: hard sync and pitch link (II
keeps an interval to I). An "Ultra LFO": one rate knob driving two waveform selectors (Wave I, Wave
II, each off plus seven related shapes), with switches routing each destination to Wave I or II. A
PT2399-style delay (LFO on its time) into a reverb, a dry/wet each. Patch points for CV and
self-patching. Players love its immediacy and that every value has a physical place.

**Moog Subharmonicon.** Two oscillators, each with two subharmonic oscillators that divide its
frequency (1/1 to 1/16), mixed by level; a ladder filter with envelopes; two 4-step sequencers and
polyrhythmic clock dividers. Its signature is subharmonic, just-intonation-like harmony and
polyrhythm. Dense panel, all knobs and a patch bay.

**Soma Lyra-8.** An "organismic" synth: eight voices in four pairs, each voice played by touching a
sensor plate (it fades in and out with the touch, or holds). Each voice has its own pitch; pairs share
sharpness and can modulate each other (FM between voices). A "hyper-LFO" of two LFOs combined (AND /
OR) modulates pitch. Delay with modulation and feedback, and distortion. Feedback and chaos are part of
its character; it's played by hand, with touch and knobs, no screen or menus.

## 2. The target: the M-VAVE FM-1 (the constraint)

A small keyboard synth whose stock firmware is FM. Far fewer controls than any of the above, and a
screen:
- **8 knobs:** MASTER (a volume pot); SELECT, PRESETS, ALGORITHM (endless encoders, top left); KNOB1
  to KNOB4 (endless encoders, top right). Encoders have detents.
- **14 buttons** with lights: FX, SEL, ENV, LFO, EDIT, GLO / HOME, SAVE, ARP, SEQ, PLAY/STOP, REC, and
  OCT-, OCT+. The printed names come from the stock firmware and can't change.
- **27 touch keys** (16 white, 11 black, in groups like a piano), with lights. Velocity/pressure unknown.
- **A 240 x 240 colour screen** (small: roughly 1.3 to 1.5 inches; moving pictures at about 20 fps).
- One small mono **speaker**, a headphone output, USB (MIDI, audio), MIDI in. **No CV, no patch
  points.**
- A modest processor: the engine uses about 21 % of it with the default sound.

So fm1-drone can't copy a dense panel. Given that, this is what it has: read `README.md` for every
knob, page, key and button.

## 3. How you can test it

Set up the shell first (in every command; exports don't persist between calls):

```
cd ~/fm1-drone
export PATH="$PWD/.venv/bin:$PATH" SDKROOT="$(xcrun --sdk macosx --show-sdk-path)"
```

**Host simulator** (the whole app, scripted; writes WAVs and PNG screenshots):
```
sh host/build_host.sh
build/host/omni_host SCRIPT.omni OUTDIR
```
Script commands (examples in `tests/scenarios/*.omni`): `tap BTN`, `press BTN` / `release BTN` (a
hold over 400 ms is a peek, under is a tap), `spin K1 N` (exact detents), `turn K1 N MS` (N detents MS
apart, real timing), `tapkey b0..b10 / w0..w15`, `key b4 down|up`, `wait MS`, `shot FILE.png`,
`wav FILE.wav` ... `wavstop`, `expect parN VALUE`, `expect view N`, probes `expect pitch0 / cut0 /
level0 / lfo0 0` (they print the value). Buttons: FX SEL ENV LFO EDIT GLO HOME SAVE ARP SEQ PLAY REC
OCT- OCT+; encoders K1..K4, SELECT, ALGO, PRESET. Parameter numbers: see the comment at the top of
`tests/scenarios/drone.omni` and the enums in `firmware/src/dsp/drone.h`.

**Browser emulator** (the real UI): `sh web/emu/build.sh` builds `build/emu/`. Serve it with
`python3 -m http.server 8767 -d build/emu` (in the background) and drive it with headless Chrome over
the DevTools protocol; a working example to copy:
`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-evanyerburgh-WeBuddhist-app/2fc2c5b0-3ffe-4df5-ab24-33e9a7f25547/scratchpad/shoot.mjs`
(switches it on, sends keys, turns knobs with `turn(knobs.KNOB2, n)`, takes screenshots). The on-page
guide under the device is part of what you're judging. **Stop every server and Chrome you start when
you're done** (`pkill -f "http.server 8767"`).

**Listening:** you can't hear, so analyse WAVs with numpy (`.venv/bin/python`): levels, spectra,
movement over time, clicks.

## 4. What to examine

Focus on the experience, given the FM-1's form factor:
- **Playability:** playing it live, slowly, over long stretches: what's under the hand, what takes too
  long to reach, two-handed use, performance gestures, stage use (dark, standing).
- **Intuitiveness:** could a drone player guess it? What would confuse them? What does the screen tell
  them, and is it readable?
- **Layout and menu diving:** how many steps to the things you use most; whether pages are worth
  their place; the tap / hold / tap-again page system; the two layers of black keys.
- **Placement and information architecture:** is each option on the right control and in the right
  page or group? Do related things sit together? Are the most-used options the most reachable? Are
  any options misplaced, duplicated, missing, or not worth having? Do the printed button names fight
  what they do, and how much does that matter?
- **Sound, briefly:** anything a drone player would miss or find wrong, measured where you can.
