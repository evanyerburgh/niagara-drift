# fm1-drone: independent design review

Reviewed 2026-10-10 from the point of view of a long-form drone player: someone who has spent years with the DS-2, Lyra-8, Subharmonicon, shruti box, tanpura and modular drone patches. Every claim is marked by how I know it:

- **[tested]**: I ran it in the host simulator or the browser emulator and measured the audio or looked at screenshots.
- **[code]**: I read it in the firmware.
- **[opinion]**: my judgement as a player.

Nothing here has touched real FM-1 hardware.

---

## 1. Summary: the five findings that matter most

1. **The core sound and the Morph idea are right.** [tested] I toggled every switch (partner, sub octave, HP, hard sync, cross-mod, OCT+) at the default Morph and at the shortest one (0.02 s). None of them made a click; the sound just moved. The defaults sit at a usable level (−12 dBFS RMS, peak −2 dBFS) and the stereo image stays safe on one speaker. For drone, "nothing ever jumps" is the most important promise an instrument can make, and this one keeps it. Keep it as the identity of the instrument.
2. **The cutoff is treated as a minor knob, but it is the second most important control.** [tested + code] Pitch gets the careful, continuous treatment (each detent glides; 12 slow detents gave exactly +720 cents with no stair steps). Cutoff gets none of it. It has 100 steps of about 1.2 semitones each, smoothed over a fixed ~60 ms. At high resonance the filter sings, so a slow turn plays a staircase of notes, not a sweep. A full sweep takes about 5 turns if you turn slowly. A fast turn jumps 6 steps (about 7 semitones) per detent. For a drone player the slow filter sweep *is* the performance, so this needs the same treatment as pitch.
3. **The motion loop has a seam that breaks long drones.** [tested] I recorded a slow 600-cent pitch rise and looped it. At every pass the pitch fell back 600 cents in about 0.1–0.2 s (5600 → 5176 → 5000 in two 100 ms probes). The cutoff does the same (85 → 65 at once in the repo's own test). Add the 30-second limit, and the loop turns a drone into an audible repeating swoop. Long-form players want a loop that returns smoothly or goes back and forth.
4. **Time scales are too short for long-form playing.** [code] Morph tops out at 10 s, Fade (PLAY and scene recall) at 20 s, the loop at 30 s, and the Pulse's slowest rate is 0.25 Hz. The LFO is the exception: at 0.02 Hz (a 50 s cycle) it is right. Scene morphs of one to five minutes are the most useful long-form gesture this instrument could have, and it is nearly there.
5. **Too much is spread over too many half-empty pages.** [code + tested] There are 14 pages, giving 56 knob slots, and only 41 are used. Mix, Filter 2, Shape, LFO 2, Setup 2 and Effects 2 are each half or mostly empty. Meanwhile drone level (always used in performance) and the LFO depths (the main way to make a drone move) are each a tap away, and on stage you lose track of which page you are on. Merging pages would cut the map to about 8 and put levels nearer the hand.

## 2. Playability

**Immediacy: good.** [tested] Press PLAY and you get a full-sounding drone in about 1 s: D3 saw with a unison partner, A3 triangle with an octave partner, and a hall reverb. The pitch knobs answer at once and glide, and the readout shows note + cents ("D3 +37"). For a first touch it does what the DS-2 does: turn it on and it drones.

**Pitch: well judged.** [tested + code] 60 cents a detent, slides between detents, SEL for single cents. Twelve slow detents landed exactly on +720 cents. The full 27 Hz–1.6 kHz range is about 118 slow detents (around 6 turns) [code]. That is fine because faster turns accelerate. My one reservation [opinion]: a drone player tunes the *second* drone against the first, to a just fifth (702 cents) or a slightly beating unison. The sweet spots are a few cents from 60-cent grid points, so you will live on SEL. I would not change the step until the hardware detent count is known, but see the suggestion on just intervals.

**Cutoff: the weak spot** (see finding 2). [tested] Turning 12 slow detents 300 ms apart moved the cutoff exactly 12 steps. Twelve detents 40 ms apart jumped from 82 to 100, the top of the range. The same applies to every 0–100 knob (resonance, X-Wave, depths, mix), but cutoff and resonance are the ones you hear as pitch.

**Two hands.** [opinion, from the emulator layout] The left hand has the LFO encoders and the left half of the black keys. The right hand has KNOB 1–4 *and* all the page buttons. On stage the right hand keeps leaving the knobs to change page. That is the opposite of the DS-2, where both hands sit on knobs. Peeking (hold page button + turn) needs two fingers of the same hand, or crossing hands. It works, but it is awkward for long, slow moves where you want to hold a knob for a minute.

**Performance gestures.**

| Gesture | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Morph on switches | Excellent: clean at every setting I tried | tested |
| Scenes (16, morphing recall) | The best idea in the design for long-form. Held back by the 20 s Fade and by sharing Fade with PLAY | tested + code |
| Turning a knob during a scene morph stops it | Good: you can catch it part way | tested (repo test) |
| Motion loop | Seam swoop, 30 s limit; switches and scenes not recorded | tested |
| Pulse | Clean gate, no clicks (largest sample step 0.095, below the plain drone's 0.12). More useful as a slow "breath" than as rhythm, but its slowest rate is 0.25 Hz | tested + code |
| OCT± | Slides over Morph: nice, no click | tested |
| PLAY fade out with tails ringing | Good stage ending | code + repo test |

**Compared with the DS-2.** [opinion] The DS-2's advantage is not features, it's that every value has a physical position you can see and grab blind. Here you get four live values plus three LFO encoders. That is fine for the first ten minutes. It gets frustrating when, during a long piece, you want "a little more resonance on II and a little less LFO on I" and that is two page changes. The fix is not more knobs; it's better choices about which pairs share a page (section 6).

## 3. Intuitiveness

**What a new player would guess:**

- KNOB 1–2 = drone I, 3–4 = drone II, with amber and teal everywhere. [tested] This is the strongest piece of the design and it is consistent on every screen I captured.
- PLAY, OCT±, SAVE, and the white keys as scenes.

**What they would not guess:**

- **Button names.** [opinion] ENV opens the Filter page (there is no envelope), EDIT opens Osc, SEQ opens Partner, GLO opens Setup, ARP opens the Pulse. The FM-1's printed legends can't change, so the screen must carry the real name. It does in the top-left corner, but in small grey type (about 9 px). A sticker overlay or a printed card would help more than anything in firmware.
- **Tap vs hold vs tap-again.** Three behaviours on one button, plus ARP, which breaks the pattern (tap = toggle, hold = page). [code] It is learnable, but the second pages are invisible until you find them. Nothing on screen says "tap again for Filter 2". A small "1/2" page dot would fix that.
- **Black keys that change meaning on the LFO pages.** [code] Same physical key, different function, chosen by the page. On a dark stage you will hit "HP filter" when you meant "Filter wave". The LFO layer's three-state route keys (dark / lit / winking) are clever, but a wink is hard to read at a glance [opinion].
- **Hidden gestures.** Holding LFO reset for half a second to lock to the Pulse, and SAVE + white key to store, are both documented only in the guide. The SAVE + key one is standard on synths. The half-second hold is not, and the lock has no visible feedback except the key's light [code].
- **An empty key.** [code] Drone I's third black key does nothing on the normal layer, while drone II's third key is pitch link. That asymmetry will confuse people.

**The screen.** [tested, screenshots in `pages/`, `drone/`, `contact.png`] The pictures are good: filter curves, a moving wave with an X-Wave marker, echo bars, pulse shape, live scopes on Play. The style is calm, which suits drone. Two problems:

- **Small text.** At 240×240 px, labels like "beats 1.3/s", "rate 22s", "driven" and the routing list on the LFO page are 7–9 px. If the FM-1's panel is around 1.3–1.5 inches (my estimate, unconfirmed), that is about 1 mm-high type. You can't read it on a floor-level stage.
- **The LFO page shows the routing as a list of words.** "I cutoff / II cutoff / I pitch…" is the hardest page to read, and it's the page where the screen matters most because routing is otherwise invisible.

## 4. Features

**DS-2 must-haves.** [code]

| DS-2 feature | Here | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Two voices, pitch, X-WAVE, PWM, osc/sub mix, sub −1/−2 | Yes | plus partner oscillators |
| 12 dB LP/HP filter, resonance to self-oscillation, no bass loss | Yes | bass kept within 1.5 dB (repo test) |
| Osc and filter LFO depths (bipolar), osc → pitch or X-WAVE | Yes | plus filter → resonance, which reviewers asked for |
| Ultra LFO: one rate, two waves, per-destination I/II | Yes | plus I+II, random, rate mod, lock |
| Hard sync, pitch link | Yes | sync keeps drone I sounding (better than the DS-2) |
| Delay (unsynced, runaway) and 8 reverbs | Delay yes. 4 reverbs, not 8 | fine |
| Volume pushing into overdrive | Per-drone Drive on the Filter page; MASTER is a plain volume pot | the DS-2's "one knob from clean to dirty" feel is gone [opinion] |
| CV / patch points | No (no hardware) | MIDI tuning per channel is a fair stand-in |

**Additions that earn their place:** [opinion] Morph, scenes, partner oscillators, drift (up to 8 cents: subtle and right), the random and random-glide LFO shapes, resonance as an LFO destination, LFO rate mod.

**Additions that don't, yet:**

- **The motion loop**, in its current form (seam, 30 s, knobs only). [tested] For drone, one long scene-to-scene morph does what most players want from it.
- **The Pulse as a rhythmic chopper.** [opinion] Drone players rarely want this, while a *slow swell* (shruti bellows, tanpura cycle) is something they reach for all the time. The engine already has a raised-cosine shape; it just needs to go slower than 4 s a cycle.
- **Swing on the Pulse.** It is a rhythm feature on a drone instrument. It's harmless, but it takes a knob slot.

**What a drone player would reach for and miss:** [opinion]

- **Just intervals.** The partner intervals are equal-tempered semitones (−12, −7, −5, 0, +3, +4, +5, +7, +12, +19 [code]). A just fifth or major third against a tempered one is the difference between "still" and "beating". Tanpura and shruti players tune just. Offer just ratios, with Detune still adding beating on purpose.
- **Subharmonics.** The Subharmonicon's whole appeal is 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5 of a pitch. The sub here is −1 or −2 octaves only. One more key state (1/3, 1/5) would open up a lot.
- **A freeze or hold for the pitch knobs**, so a bumped encoder during a 20-minute piece doesn't detune you. A "lock pitches" switch would do.
- **Long scene morphs** (minutes), separate from the PLAY fade.

**Sound issues measured:**

- **Hard sync aliasing.** [tested] With drone II synced to D3 at a non-integer ratio and the filter open, 27 % of the energy above 3 kHz and 52 % above 6 kHz sat off the harmonic series. That is aliasing, about −20 dB below the total. It's audible as a thin whistle on bright sync sweeps. It's already listed as known; worth fixing before release, because sync sweeps are a signature DS-2 gesture.
- **Bass on the built-in speaker.** [tested] In the default sound, 46 % of the energy is under 200 Hz and 16 % under 100 Hz. Mostly that is the sub at 73 Hz plus the D3 fundamental. On a small speaker, much of that is lost or turns into rattling. Raising the default an octave helped. A speaker-aware EQ, which Felucca already has, would help more.
- **Switch clicks:** none. [tested] (See finding 1.)

## 5. Layout

**Mapping to the FM-1.** [code + emulator screenshot `emu/emulator.png`]

- **LFO on SELECT / ALGORITHM / PRESETS.** Good: it's the one global section and it sits by itself. The physical order is odd, though: SELECT top-right, PRESETS bottom-left, ALGORITHM bottom-right. Wave I and Wave II end up diagonal from the rate. Nothing can be done about the hardware, but the guide and screen should draw them in their physical positions.
- **Black keys grouped 3+2 / 3+2 / 1.** This follows the keyboard's natural clusters, which makes it easy to learn by feel. Good.
- **White keys as 16 scenes.** The right use for them.

**Page grouping.** [opinion] The pages follow the DS-2's *sections* (osc, filter, LFO). A performer thinks in *what moves together*. Some current pairings work against that:

- **Level** sits behind HOME on a page with two empty knobs. Balancing the two drones is a constant performance act.
- **Resonance** is on a different page from cutoff. The two are used together constantly.
- **Width (PWM)** has its own half-empty page.
- **Filter 2** holds a single knob (X-mod amount).

**Names.** [opinion] The FM-1 legends can't change, so name pages after the legend where possible, or use a cheap overlay. ENV → Filter is the worst mismatch: anyone with synth experience will look for an envelope.

**Screen.** [tested] Concept D is attractive and calm. The bottom value row is the clearest part. The top pictures are pretty but often carry information in tiny type. Bigger, fewer words; let the curves carry it.

## 6. Suggestions

| # | Change | Problem it solves | Cost | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Give cutoff (and resonance) the pitch treatment: finer internal steps (e.g. 0–1000), each detent gliding over the time since the last, gentler acceleration | Stair-stepped resonant sweeps; 5 turns for a full sweep | Medium | **High** |
| 2 | Motion loop: glide back to the start over the Morph time at the seam, or add a back-and-forth mode; raise the limit to a few minutes | Swoop at every pass | Small–medium | **High** |
| 3 | A separate Scene morph time reaching 5 min; PLAY fade up to 60 s | Long-form transitions | Small | **High**, *Evan's decision* (another knob, likely on Setup) |
| 4 | Merge pages: Play = Pitch, Cutoff ×2; Play 2 = Reso, Level ×2; Filter = Drive, Width, X-mod; LFO page unchanged; fold LFO 2, Setup 2 and Effects 2 into fewer pages | Half-empty pages; level and resonance too far away | Medium | **High**, *Evan's decision* |
| 5 | Just-interval option for partner intervals and pitch link (one global key or Setup choice: Equal / Just) | Tempered fifths and thirds beat; drone players tune just | Small | Medium |
| 6 | Subharmonic choices for the sub (1/3, 1/5) as more states on the sub key | Missing Subharmonicon colour | Small–medium | Medium |
| 7 | Pulse: slowest rate around 0.03 Hz (30 s); rename to "Swell" or similar; make swing secondary | Pulse built for rhythm, drone players want breath | Small | Medium, *Evan's decision* on name |
| 8 | Band-limit hard sync | About −20 dB aliasing on bright sync | Medium | Medium (before release) |
| 9 | Page indicator ("Filter 1/2") and bigger page name; bump smallest screen text to at least 12 px | Hidden second pages; text unreadable on stage | Small | Medium |
| 10 | Show LFO routing as a picture (lines from Wave I / II to the four destinations) instead of words | Hardest page to read | Medium | Medium |
| 11 | Use drone I's empty black key (normal layer): e.g. "lock pitches" against bumps, or move pitch link there for symmetry | Asymmetric dead key | Small | Low, *Evan's decision* |
| 12 | Printed overlay or card for the button legends | ENV / EDIT / SEQ / GLO don't describe their pages | Small (not firmware) | Medium |
| 13 | A "MASTER drive" curve: the last part of a global gain knob pushes into the master clip, as the DS-2's Volume does | Lost one-knob overdrive gesture | Small | Low |
| 14 | Confirm the speaker EQ on the unit; consider a gentle low cut only on the speaker path | 46 % of the default sound under 200 Hz | Small | After hardware |

## 7. What I tested

All files are in the review folder (`.../scratchpad/review/`).

- **Unit tests:** `tests/run_tests.sh` passed in full (the update-loader test was skipped; it needs the SDK).
- **Repo scenarios re-run:** `drone.omni` → `drone/`, `pages.omni` → `pages/` (all checks OK). The screenshots are collected in `contact.png`.
- **My scenarios** in `sc/`, with WAVs in `out/`:
  - `switches.omni`: each switch at default and minimum Morph, with clicks and level dips measured by `an1.py`. No clicks.
  - `default.omni`: default sound level, bass share, stereo correlation; Pulse at full gate checked for clicks (`an1.py`).
  - `stairs.omni` and `q.omni`: slow and fast cutoff and pitch turns. Pitch was +720 cents exactly. Cutoff was 1 step per slow detent, and 12 fast detents reached the cap. The spectral tracking in `an2.py` / `an3.py` was too noisy to plot the stairs, so that claim rests on the step counts and the code's 60 ms smoothing.
  - `sync.omni` + `an4.py`: hard-sync aliasing (energy off the harmonic series).
  - `loopseam2.omni`: drone I's pitch probed every 100 ms through two loop passes. It drops 600 cents at each seam. (`loopseam.omni` / `an5.py` was a first attempt whose pitch tracker failed; ignore it.)
- **Browser emulator:** built it, served it on port 8766, and drove it with headless Chrome (`emu/shoot.mjs`): PLAY, a switch, storing a scene, knob turns. Screenshots are `emu/emulator.png` and `emu/controls.png` (the on-page guide). The server and Chrome are stopped.
- **Code read:** `firmware/src/app/ui.c` (control map, acceleration, pages, keys) and `firmware/src/dsp/drone.c` (parameter ranges, fade, morph, cutoff smoothing, intervals).
- `~/fm1-drone` was left unchanged on `main`.
