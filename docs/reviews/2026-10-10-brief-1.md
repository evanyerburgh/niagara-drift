# fm1-drone: briefing for an independent design review

You are reviewing **fm1-drone**, open-source firmware that turns the M-VAVE FM-1 (a small keyboard
synth) into a **drone synthesizer** inspired by the Stylophone CPM DS-2. The owner, Evan, is a product
designer (not a developer). He wants an **unbiased critique** of playability, intuitiveness, features
and layout, with concrete suggestions. You have no stake in the current design: say plainly what
doesn't work, and also what does.

Code: `~/fm1-drone` (read `CLAUDE.md` there for decisions and history; `README.md` for the player's
view). Today is 2026-10-10. Nothing has run on real hardware yet; the unit arrives ~21 October.

---

## 1. The inspiration: Stylophone CPM DS-2

An analog drone synth (~€200–260, Eurorack-compatible, battery-powered, built-in speaker). Everything
is a dedicated knob or switch on a dense panel: **no menus, no screen**.

**Two identical drone voices**, each with:
- **PITCH** knob (27 Hz–1.6 kHz, continuous; an analog knob with a long sweep).
- **X-WAVE**: one knob blending square → triangle → saw. **PWM** acts on the square.
- **OSC MIX**: oscillator ↔ square sub; a switch puts the sub −1 or −2 octaves.
- **Filter**: 12 dB state-variable, LP/HP switch, cutoff, resonance to self-oscillation ("no bass
  loss" as resonance rises), its own bipolar LFO depth.
- **Oscillator LFO depth** (bipolar) with a switch: pitch or X-WAVE.
- **VOLUME**: pushes into a pleasant overdrive past ~3 o'clock.

**Between the voices:** HARD SYNC (II to I; on the DS-2 this silences oscillator I, which reviewers
disliked), PITCH LINK (II keeps a constant interval to I).

**Ultra LFO:** one RATE knob (0.02–70 Hz) driving two waveform selectors, WAVE I and WAVE II (off +
seven shapes each; II's are relatives of I's: 90° shifted, double speed, exponential versions). Rate
lights show both. Routing switches: osc I, osc II, filter I, filter II each pick WAVE I or II; both can
hit one destination. Delay time has its own LFO depth and I/II pick.

**Effects:** PT2399-style delay (70–450 ms, repeats to self-oscillation, tape-like grit, LFO-modulated
time; deliberately unsynced: "use your ears"), then reverb (8 types: 3 rooms, 2 plates, 2 halls, chorus
hall), each with a dry/wet. Aux input. Mono or stereo out.

**Patch points** (12–14): pitch CV, cutoff CV, PWM, LFO rate and reset, outputs. Used for keyboard
playing (pitch + gate into cutoff = low-pass gate), self-patching (LFO into its own rate), FM.

**What reviewers loved:** immediacy (turn it on and drone), every control in reach, the LFO pairs,
pitch link keeping things musical, the overdrive, the delay going "juicy" into the reverb, slow
meditative micro-adjustment ("you can get lost in it"). **What they missed:** a true random LFO,
resonance modulation, sync not killing osc I, a clocked delay (Stylophone says no on purpose), an
attenuator on gates, envelopes (out of scope).

## 2. The target: M-VAVE FM-1 hardware

Not a DS-2: far fewer controls, a screen, digital.
- **8 knobs:** MASTER (volume pot); SELECT, ALGORITHM, PRESETS (endless encoders, top-left);
  KNOB1–4 (endless encoders, top-right). Encoders have detents (exact count per turn unconfirmed).
- **14 buttons:** FX, SEL, ENV, LFO, EDIT, GLO / HOME, SAVE, ARP, SEQ, PLAY/STOP, REC; OCT−, OCT+.
  Buttons have LEDs.
- **27 touch keys** (F3–G5): 16 white, 11 black, with LEDs. Pressure/velocity unknown.
- **240×240 colour LCD** (slow SPI, ~20 fps for moving pictures).
- One small built-in **speaker** (mono, weak bass), headphone out (stereo unconfirmed), USB-C (MIDI,
  audio), TRS MIDI in. No CV.
- JieLi AC79 SoC: modest CPU. The engine measures ~20 % with defaults, ~27 % with everything on.

## 3. The design as it stands

Principles Evan has set (see `CLAUDE.md` decisions):
- What's used most in performance is always live; the rest is one tap away. Avoid menu diving.
- **KNOB1–2 are always drone I, KNOB3–4 always drone II**, on every page.
- Page buttons: **tap** to stay on a page, **hold** to peek (turn, let go, back). Tap again for a
  page's second page.
- No on/off switches on knobs; switches live on the black keys (lit = on), and the keys follow the page
  (a normal layer and an LFO layer).
- Two drones (like the DS-2), each with a **partner oscillator** at an interval (unison, fifth,
  octave…) with detune, so it sounds richer than two oscillators.
- Pitch: **60 cents per encoder detent**, faster turns move further; each detent's change slides over
  the time since the last detent so turns sound continuous; hold SEL for single cents. (Under review
  once the hardware's detent count is known.)
- **Morph:** buttons and keys never jump; the sound moves to the new setting over a Morph time
  (0.02–10 s). Knobs follow the hand.
- Screen: top 2/3 a picture of what the page's knobs do (waveforms, filter curves, LFO, echoes…);
  bottom row the four knob values, the one being turned lit. No interval readout ("tune by ear").
- Dark, glowing look; drone I amber, drone II teal, LFO purple, FX blue, Pulse pink.

The full current control map (page map, top knobs, black keys on both layers, scenes, ARP, REC) is
in **`~/fm1-drone/README.md` → "Playing it"**. Read it. Additions beyond the DS-2: the Pulse (both
drones chopped in time; ARP), a motion loop (REC), 16 scenes on the white keys with morphing recall,
LFO rate mod and LFO lock to the Pulse, random LFO shapes, resonance as an LFO destination.

Open questions Evan knows about: the 60-cents-per-detent pitch feel; a MIDI low-pass-gate mode (later);
whether the headphone jack is stereo.

## 4. How you can test it

Set up the shell first (every command; exports don't persist between calls):

```
cd ~/fm1-drone
export PATH="$PWD/.venv/bin:$PATH" SDKROOT="$(xcrun --sdk macosx --show-sdk-path)"
```

**Host simulator** (the whole app, scripted; writes WAVs and PNG screenshots):
```
sh host/build_host.sh
build/host/omni_host SCRIPT.omni OUTDIR
```
Script commands (see `tests/scenarios/drone.omni` and `pages.omni` for examples): `tap BTN`,
`press BTN` / `release BTN` (a hold over 400 ms is a peek; under 400 ms is a tap), `spin K1 N` (exact
detents), `turn K1 N MS` (N detents, MS apart, with the speed curve), `tapkey b0..b10 / w0..w15`,
`key b4 down|up`, `wait MS`, `shot FILE.png`, `wav FILE.wav` … `wavstop`, `expect parN VALUE`,
`expect view N`, `midi B1 B2 B3`. Buttons: FX SEL ENV LFO EDIT GLO HOME SAVE ARP SEQ PLAY REC OCT- OCT+;
encoders K1–K4, SELECT, ALGO, PRESET. Parameter numbers are listed at the top of `drone.omni`.

**Browser emulator** (the real UI, playable): `sh web/emu/build.sh` builds `build/emu/`. Serve it with
`python3 -m http.server 8766 -d build/emu` (run it in the background) and drive it with headless
Chrome over the DevTools protocol: a working example is
`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-evanyerburgh-WeBuddhist-app/2fc2c5b0-3ffe-4df5-ab24-33e9a7f25547/scratchpad/shoot.mjs`
(switches it on, sends keys, turns knobs with `turn(knobs.KNOB2, n)`, takes screenshots). The page's
own guide under the device is also worth judging. **Stop every server and Chrome you start when
you're done** (`pkill -f "http.server 8766"`).

**Listening:** you can't hear, so analyse WAVs with numpy (`.venv/bin/python`): level, spectrum,
brightness over time, clicks (sample-to-sample steps), etc.

**Unit tests:** `sh tests/run_tests.sh` (all should pass).

## 6. What to deliver

Write `REVIEW.md` in the review folder, for Evan (plain language, no code jargon), with:

1. **Summary**: the 5 most important findings, best first.
2. **Playability**: playing it live as a drone instrument: immediacy, what's fast or slow to reach,
   two-handed use, performance gestures (Morph, scenes, motion loop, pulse), and how it compares with
   the DS-2's everything-in-reach panel.
3. **Intuitiveness**: could a new player guess it? Page map, the knob pairs, tap/hold/peek, black-key
   layers, three-state keys, hidden gestures (hold to lock, SAVE + key), what the screen tells you.
4. **Features**: the DS-2's must-haves present or missing; additions that earn their place and ones
   that don't; anything that's confusing or redundant; sound issues you measured.
5. **Layout**: the mapping of functions to the FM-1's knobs, buttons and keys; grouping; naming (are
   the button names FX/ENV/EDIT/SEQ/GLO a good fit for what they now do?); the screen design.
6. **Suggestions**: concrete changes, each with the problem it solves, its cost (small / medium /
   large), and its priority. Note any that would need Evan's design decision.
7. **What you tested**: a short list (scripts, measurements, screenshots), with file names in the
   review folder.

Keep it readable: short sections, tables where they help, around 1,500–2,500 words.
