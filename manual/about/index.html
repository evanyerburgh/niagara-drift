Niagara Drift

About

Two drones that breathe and drift on their own, as open-source firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1

What it does

Sound

  • 2 dronesVirtual-analog: each a wave from sine to square, a partner (a second oscillator) at an interval, a sub and noise, into its own resonant filter and drive
  • 11 tunings12-tone to Partch's 43 notes, La Monte Young's, Harrison's, the shrutis; intervals named in each one's terms
  • FM cross-modFM between the drones: each bending the other's pitch, or the finished sound bending both (Snarl); or drone I moving drone II's filter
  • Feedback and CharacterThe finished sound fed back into the drones; the oscillators from clean to vintage
  • 6 delays, 5 reverbsClean to tape and self-warping echoes, a drive after them, reverbs from a room to a freeze

Movement

  • Ultra LFO2 waves of 10 shapes each, on each drone's pitch, wave, cutoff or resonance, and the delay
  • MovementOne knob for every LFO's depth at once, from still to double
  • SwellA tremolo on both drones, in time: from a soft swell to a hard gate
  • Moves on its ownDrift (slow random modulation), Wander (a random melody on a scale) and Age (tape wear)

Playing

  • 16 presetsEach a different side of the instrument, each moving over a minute
  • 8 gesturesMacros: several settings at once on top of your sound, each in its own order; held, pressed harder, or tapped to stay
  • Save 48 soundsSix banks of eight scenes, yours to fill; each glides in, or crossfades as you press its key harder
  • Motion loopRecords your knob moves, gestures and scenes, and plays them back
  • Never jumpsEverything glides (morphs): buttons, keys and scenes move to new settings over times you set

Connect

  • MIDI inFollows a MIDI clock over USB or TRS: the Swell, the LFO, the delay, Wander and the scenes keep its time; notes on channels 1 and 2 tune the drones
  • Hear the screenWith the FM-1 Screen Reader, it says what its screen shows, for players who can't see it
  • Play it in a browserThe emulator on the front page is the firmware itself, with sound; no FM-1 needed
  • Install from a browserInstall, update, back up and go back to M-VAVE's firmware from Chrome or Edge

Technical details

Sound

Audio44.1 kHz stereo; 32-bit floating point inside, 24-bit out; a block every 5.8 ms
OscillatorsVirtual analog, 4 shapes blended continuously (sine, triangle, saw, square) with pulse width; saw and pulse band-limited (polyBLEP); hard sync; cross-FM
PartnerA second oscillator on each drone: an octave or a fifth or fourth below, unison, a third, fourth or fifth above, an octave, or an octave and a fifth; each tempered or just (pure, beatless); detune up to 30 cents for slow beating
Sub and noiseA sub of 4 waves (square, sine, triangle, saw), 1–4 octaves down or the subharmonics ⅓ and ⅕; under a low drone the lowest become ticks and pulses (recipe); noise from white to pink
FilterA state-variable filter (zero-delay feedback, after Andrew Simper): low-, band- or high-pass, resonant to self-oscillation; an antialiased drive
EffectsA delay of 70 ms to 1.46 s in six types, from clean to tape; a drive between the delay and the reverb; a reverb of eight lines, 0.3 to 40 s or a freeze, in five types (Space and Details)

Movement and tuning

LFO2 waves of 10 shapes each (20 in all: triangles, squares, saws, exponentials, patterns, random, AND and OR steps), one cycle in 100 s to 70 Hz, on pitch, wave, cutoff, resonance and the delay
TuningA from 427 to 453 Hz (440 to start, 432 for those who like it), 1 Hz a click or 0.1 with SEL; 12-tone, quarter tones, 19-, 31- and 53-tone, just, the harmonics, LMY, Harrison, the shrutis, Partch
DetuningEach drone a cent at a time off its Tuning's notes (SEL + a pitch knob), kept as you move between notes; each partner up to 30 cents, for beating; Drift lets the detune wander

Playing and connecting

Polyphonic aftertouchThe scene keys' and keys 9–12's own pressure, read as they're held: scenes crossfade and the pressure gestures follow it; the touch is a setting (Setup 2); sent out over USB too (MIDI in)
MIDIIn, over USB and TRS: notes on channels 1 and 2 tune the drones; the clock (24 pulses a beat) and Start, with Sync on or off (MIDI in)

Storage and license

Storage6 banks of 8 scenes and 16 presets in the FM-1's flash; backups to a file before every install; a restore that a power cut can't break (Help)
LicenseGPL-3.0, source on GitHub

Where it came from

Named for Day of Niagara (the Theatre of Eternal Music, 1965) and La Monte Young's Drift Studies. It began from ideas in the Stylophone CPM DS-2, the Moog Subharmonicon and the Soma Lyra-8, and has grown into an instrument of its own.
